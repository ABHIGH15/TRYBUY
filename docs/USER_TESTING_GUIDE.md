# TRYBUY — Qualitative Validation Sprint (Day 1)

## 1. The Experiment Methodology (Path 2)
**Format:** Small Moderated Comparison Study
**Sample Size:** 5–10 users (target mix: heavy online shoppers, fashion/lifestyle buyers).
**Protocol:** Within-subject design. Each user will experience both the Control (standard save) and Treatment (Smart Save) flows to compare friction vs. perceived value.
**Core Objective:** Determine if the friction of explicit intent capture is justified by the downstream value of differentiated re-engagement and contextual recall.

---

## 2. The Hypotheses Being Tested

### Layer 1: Capture Hypothesis (Friction vs. Value)
> "Users presented with the Smart Save modal will complete the save at a high rate, and the majority will choose an intent rather than selecting 'Skip for now'."
*Look for:* Frustration, speed of selection, whether they intuitively understand the categories.

### Layer 2: Recall Hypothesis (Memory Value)
> "When viewing TryBuy Memory, users will articulate that the context (intent chips/groupings) makes the list more useful than a standard flat wishlist."
*Look for:* Do they actually read the intent badges? Does it change how they talk about the saved items?

---

## 3. Moderated Interview Script

### Introduction (5 mins)
*"Hi! I'm testing an early prototype for a new shopping experience. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm going to give you a few tasks, but mostly I just want you to think out loud as you click around. If something feels annoying or confusing, please tell me—that's exactly what I'm looking for."*

### Task 1: Baseline Browsing (Control)
*Make sure the Prototype Lab is set to **Control** before starting.*
1. "Imagine you're casually browsing for clothes. Open the app and scroll through the feed."
2. "Find an item you kind of like, but wouldn't buy today. Tap the heart to save it."
3. "Navigate to the Memory tab (bookmark icon). What do you see here? Does this match how you usually save things online?"

### Task 2: The Smart Save Intervention (Treatment)
*Navigate to `/internal-lab` and switch the variant to **Treatment**.*
1. "Go back to the Discover feed. Find another item you're interested in, but maybe you think it's too expensive, or you want to look for similar items first."
2. "Save that item."
*(Observe: Do they read the modal? Do they hesitate? Which option do they pick, or do they skip?)*
3. "Tell me what just happened. How did that feel compared to the first time you saved an item?"

### Task 3: The Recall & Re-engagement Value
1. "Go back to the Memory tab. You now have two items saved. How do you feel about this layout?"
2. "If you came back to this app next week, would having that extra label (e.g., 'Waiting for a price drop') be helpful to you, or is it just clutter?"
*(Navigate to `/internal-lab` and hit 'Simulate Future Event')*
3. "You just got a notification/alert. Let's look at the top of the Memory page. What is this telling you? Is this useful, or is it annoying?"

### Debrief (5 mins)
1. "If Myntra or Amazon added that 'Why are you saving this?' popup every time you liked an item, would you use it, skip it, or hate it?"
2. "Was there a reason you saved an item today that *wasn't* listed in those 4 options?"
3. "Overall, do you prefer the first way you saved, or the second way?"

## 4. Post-Session Data Capture
After each session, log the following in your spreadsheet:
- **Participant ID:** (e.g., P1, P2)
- **Save Completion:** (Did they abandon the save when the modal appeared?)
- **Intent Chosen:** (Price, Compare, Later, Explore, or Skipped)
- **Taxonomy Gaps:** (Did they mention an intent we don't have, like 'Gifting'?)
- **Qualitative Friction:** (High / Medium / Low)
- **Perceived Value of Memory:** (High / Medium / Low)
- **Key Quote:** (One verbatim quote that stood out)
