# TryBuy: User Interview & Survey Guide

**Objective:** Discover whether the "I like this, but I'm not ready to buy" problem exists naturally, how users currently manage unresolved shopping intent, and what triggers them to eventually purchase or abandon a saved item.

**Core Principle:** Avoid hypothetical questions ("Would you use this feature?"). Focus entirely on *actual recent behaviour*.

---

## Part 1: Screener Questionnaire (Survey format)
*Use these to recruit the right participants. Target: Gen Z discovery-heavy shoppers, frequent browsers, and users who maintain large wishlists.*

1. **How often do you browse e-commerce apps (like Myntra, Amazon, Ajio) without the immediate intention of buying anything?**
   *   [ ] Daily
   *   [ ] A few times a week
   *   [ ] A few times a month
   *   [ ] Rarely / Only when I need something specific (Disqualify/Deprioritize)

2. **Roughly how many items would you estimate are currently in your wishlists or "save for later" across all your shopping apps?**
   *   [ ] 0-10
   *   [ ] 10-50
   *   [ ] 50-100+
   *   [ ] I don't use wishlists (Disqualify/Deprioritize)

3. **What is your primary method for remembering a product you like but aren't ready to buy yet?**
   *   [ ] App Wishlist/Save feature
   *   [ ] Add to Cart and leave it
   *   [ ] Take a screenshot
   *   [ ] Send link to myself/friend
   *   [ ] I just try to remember it

---

## Part 2: Qualitative Interview Guide (1-on-1 Sessions)

### Introduction & Warm-up (5 mins)
*   "We are researching how people shop online, specifically what happens *before* you actually click buy."
*   "There are no right or wrong answers. I'm just interested in your actual habits."
*   "Can you open your most-used shopping app (e.g., Myntra) to help you remember recent sessions?"

### Core Behavioural Questions (The "Recent Experience" Deep Dive - 15 mins)
*Goal: Ground the conversation in reality.*

1.  **The Abandonment:** "Tell me about the last time you were browsing for fashion/lifestyle items, found something you really liked, but *didn't* end up buying it."
    *   *Probe:* What was the item?
    *   *Probe:* Why exactly didn't you buy it right then? (Listen for constraints: price, sizing, needing to compare, waiting for paycheck, etc.)
2.  **The Capture:** "What did you do with that item? How did you make sure you didn't lose it?"
    *   *Probe:* Did you wishlist it? Screenshot it? Add to cart?
    *   *Probe:* If you wishlisted it, did you add it to a specific board/list, or just a general tap of the heart?
3.  **The Re-engagement:** "Did you ever go back to that specific item?"
    *   *If yes:* What triggered you to go back? (Did the app remind you? Did you just remember? Were you bored?)
    *   *If no:* Why not? Did you forget about it, or did you lose interest?

### Context & Workflow Deep Dive (10 mins)
*Goal: Understand how they manage unresolved intent.*

4.  **The Graveyard:** "Can you open your wishlist right now? Scroll down a bit to things you added a few weeks or months ago. Pick one item."
    *   *Probe:* Do you remember exactly *why* you saved that specific item?
    *   *Probe:* Are you ever going to buy it? What would need to happen for you to buy it today?
5.  **The Comparison:** "Tell me about a time you were trying to decide between 2 or 3 similar items. How did you handle that comparison?"
    *   *Probe:* Did you use screenshots? Did you bounce between tabs? Did you add them all to cart?
6.  **The Notification:** "Think about the last time an app notified you about an item in your wishlist (e.g., a price drop). What happened?"
    *   *Probe:* Did you buy it? Why or why not? Was the notification helpful or annoying?

### Wrap-up & The "Magic Wand" (5 mins)
7.  "If you had a magic wand and could change one thing about how apps let you save or remember products you're interested in, what would it be?"

## Part 3: What We Are Listening For (Synthesis Checklist)
After conducting 5-10 interviews, evaluate the responses against these hypotheses:

*   [ ] **The "Lost Context" Problem:** Do users forget *why* they wishlisted something after a few weeks?
*   [ ] **The "Cart as Wishlist" Hack:** Are users using the Cart because the Wishlist feels too disconnected or buried?
*   [ ] **The Constraint Variety:** Is price the *only* reason they wait, or are comparison, timing, and occasion equally common?
*   [ ] **The Notification Fatigue:** Are users ignoring price drop alerts because they weren't waiting for a price drop in the first place?
*   [ ] **The Screenshot Workaround:** Are they using screenshots to compare items side-by-side because apps don't let them?

If the answer to most of these is YES, the "Intent-Aware Shopping Layer" hypothesis is validated.
